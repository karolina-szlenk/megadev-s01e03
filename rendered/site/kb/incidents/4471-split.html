How to tell them apart

  • month-first: the shared parser reads every date month first. Day-first tenants get day and month swapped: booked for 2 July, invite says 7 February. This is the bug #4471 names.
  • form-hint: booking-v3’s date field prints MM/DD/YYYY under it. People at day-first tenants type what the hint tells them, and the form reads it the tenant’s way. Same symptom, but the fix is in the form, not the parser. Only tenants on booking-v3, only through the form.
  • one-day: the date is not swapped; it moves by one day. Two cities, two weekdays for one meeting; an invite that arrives before the booking; “only June”. That points at time zones, not at day and month order. The desk read these as one-day moves (desk-notes.json). It counts them where their tenant files them, under the parser, and so does the table above; the parser fix will not close them.

Intake’s tag (meta.intakeTag) is a guess from the reporter’s browser and disagrees with the tenant for many reports. Counts here come from the tenant and channel, not from the tag.

What to change

  • Keep #4471 for the parser. Its count is the month-first row, not every report merged into it.
  • A subject line says what the reporter saw, not what broke. Before marking a big ticket known, read a sample of bodies and check them against the tenant and channel.
